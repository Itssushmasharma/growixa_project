# 1. Executive Summary

The IITDEVELOPER AI Visibility & Growth Platform is a subscription-based web application that helps businesses, agencies, developers, and marketing teams improve website visibility and business outcomes across traditional search engines and AI answer environments.

Unlike audit-only products, the platform is designed as a closed-loop growth system. It continuously monitors a connected website and its market, identifies the highest-value opportunities, generates exact fixes and content, requests approval where required, publishes or creates pull requests, distributes approved content, measures the outcome, and uses the evidence to plan the next action.

# 2. Product Vision and Principles

## 2.1 Vision

Make high-quality website growth execution accessible to businesses that cannot continuously coordinate a complete SEO strategist, technical specialist, content team, developer, analyst, authority manager and distribution team.

## 2.2 Product principles

| **Principle** | **Meaning** |
|----|----|
| Outcome-first | Prioritize visibility, qualified traffic, conversions and revenue-related results rather than vanity scores. |
| Execution over reporting | Every important finding must lead to an implementation-ready action, generated asset, patch or workflow. |
| Evidence before claims | Clearly separate verified external data, observed samples, internal calculated scores and AI recommendations. |
| Human control | Production publishing, outreach and high-risk technical changes require configurable approval. |
| Safe automation | Use drafts, pull requests, previews, validation, rollback and audit trails. |
| Continuous improvement | The platform should re-evaluate performance and create the next best action automatically. |
| Simple user experience | Users add a website directly after registration; an organization/workspace creation step is not required. |
| Extensible foundation | Use account, website and entitlement boundaries so teams, agencies and advanced plans can be supported later. |

# 3. Problem Statement

Most businesses use fragmented tools that identify website problems but do not complete the work needed to improve performance. Technical audits, keyword research, content creation, schema, internal links, competitor monitoring, distribution, development changes and reporting remain disconnected.

## 3.1 Current user pain points

- Audit tools produce long reports without exact implementation-ready fixes.

- Business owners do not know which recommendation will create the most value.

- SEO, content, development and analytics teams work in separate tools and queues.

- Existing content becomes outdated or loses performance without timely refreshes.

- New content is often published without topic strategy, distribution or measurement.

- AI search visibility is difficult to observe and is frequently represented with unsupported claims.

- Small businesses cannot maintain a full specialist team.

- Agencies struggle to serve many websites consistently with limited staff.

- Technical changes can introduce indexing, rendering, security or conversion risks.

## 3.2 Opportunity

A unified platform can convert website and market data into prioritized growth tasks, generate the required work, deliver it through safe publishing workflows, and measure whether each action improved performance.

# 4. Goals, Non-Goals and Success Metrics

## 4.1 Product goals

1\. Enable a user to register, subscribe and add a website without creating a separate organization or workspace.

2\. Understand each website's business context, services, entities, audience, content, technology and competitors.

3\. Continuously discover technical, content, AEO, GEO, authority, local and conversion opportunities.

4\. Prioritize work using impact, confidence, effort, risk and business relevance.

5\. Generate exact fixes, content, schema, code patches, briefs and distribution assets.

6\. Apply approved changes through CMS integrations or repository pull requests.

7\. Measure before-and-after outcomes and use results to plan subsequent work.

8\. Support several websites and team members under one account according to subscription entitlements.

9\. Give platform operators control over subscriptions, usage, models, prompts, jobs, integrations and safety policies.

## 4.2 Non-goals for initial releases

- Guaranteeing a specific Google ranking, traffic increase or AI citation.

- Mass-producing low-quality daily blog posts.

- Automatically purchasing or generating spam backlinks.

- Publishing high-risk technical changes directly to production without review.

- Replacing Google Search Console, Google Analytics or specialist rank-data providers.

- Claiming that an internal E-E-A-T, GEO or AI-readability score is an official Google or LLM platform score.

- Supporting every CMS and repository provider in the first release.

# 7. Functional Requirements

## 7.1 Authentication and account

- Support email/password registration, email verification, login, logout, password reset and session management.

- Support optional social login and enterprise SSO in later releases.

- Create an account automatically during registration; do not require a separate workspace creation screen.

- Allow an account owner to invite members and assign roles.

- Support roles: Owner, Admin, Growth Manager, Content Editor, Developer, Analyst and Viewer.

- Enforce website-level permissions where a member should not access all websites.

## 7.2 Subscription and entitlement management

- Support free trial, paid plans, plan changes, cancellation and grace periods.

- Apply entitlements for websites, crawl pages, agent runs, model credits, competitors, members, scheduled jobs and report exports.

- Provide clear usage meters and projected overage before an operation starts.

- Pause non-critical scheduled work when payment or entitlement becomes invalid.

- Never block access to previously generated reports immediately after downgrade; preserve read-only access according to policy.

## 7.3 Website onboarding and verification

- Accept root domains and validated subdomains.

- Detect redirects, canonical host, protocol and common deployment platforms.

- Verify control through DNS, HTML file, meta tag, Search Console connection or CMS integration.

- Collect business name, industry, services/products, locations, audience, conversion goals, brand voice, restricted claims and competitors.

- Allow the user to configure crawl scope, exclusions, authentication and rate limits.

## 7.4 Website crawling and understanding

- Use HTTP crawling for standard pages and browser rendering for JavaScript-dependent pages.

- Respect robots.txt by default and disclose any user-authorized override for owned websites.

- Extract status, canonical, robots directives, metadata, headings, body sections, links, media, structured data, forms, authors and update dates.

- Detect duplicates, thin pages, orphan pages, broken links, redirect chains, missing metadata and conflicting signals.

- Build a website inventory and semantic knowledge base linked to website, page, entity, topic and conversion goal.

- Store crawl snapshots so differences can be measured over time.

## 7.5 Opportunity detection and prioritization

- Generate findings for technical SEO, on-page SEO, content, AEO, GEO, local visibility, authority, distribution and conversion.

- Every finding must include evidence, affected URLs, severity, business relevance, confidence, effort, risk and recommended action.

- Calculate a priority score using configurable weighted inputs rather than relying only on an LLM.

- Allow users to change goals and weighting, such as leads, ecommerce revenue, local calls or brand visibility.

## 7.6 Action generation

- Generate implementation-ready metadata, headings, content sections, FAQs, structured data, internal links, redirect maps, robots/sitemap changes and developer tasks.

- Provide current state, proposed state, rationale, target query/topic, validation results and rollback plan.

- Support user editing, comments, approval, rejection and reassignment.

- Record the model, prompt version, evidence and source data used for each generated artifact.

## 7.7 Content strategy and production

- Create topic clusters, pillar/supporting-page plans and content calendars based on demand, business value, existing coverage and competitor gaps.

- Generate content briefs and drafts for service pages, product pages, articles, comparisons, FAQs, case studies, guides, local pages and glossary entries.

- Refresh existing content when performance declines, facts become outdated or competitors materially improve coverage.

- Apply brand voice, required facts, forbidden claims, jurisdiction and citation rules.

- Detect duplication, unsupported claims, keyword cannibalization and low-value scaled content patterns.

## 7.8 Technical implementation

- Generate validated JSON-LD for applicable schema types.

- Generate internal-link proposals with source page, destination page, context and anchor text.

- Create repository branches and pull requests for approved code changes where supported.

- Create CMS drafts for approved content and metadata changes where supported.

- Run post-deployment checks and automatically mark failed validations for rollback or manual review.

## 7.9 Distribution and authority

- Repurpose approved website content into channel-specific drafts such as LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, X, WhatsApp, email and Google Business Profile.

- Support approval and scheduling through available integrations.

- Identify legitimate directories, partnerships, resource pages, PR and outreach opportunities.

- Generate personalized outreach drafts but require approval before sending by default.

- Prohibit automated spam submissions, purchased links and deceptive authority practices.

## 7.10 Monitoring and measurement

- Monitor crawl health, indexability, rankings, impressions, clicks, CTR, conversions, content changes and competitor changes.

- Track AI visibility as sampled observations with query, engine, date, mention, citation, cited URL and competitor presence.

- Clearly label sampled observations and never present them as universal platform-wide visibility.

- Create alerts and new actions when a meaningful change, failure or opportunity is detected.

## 7.11 Reporting

- Provide executive, technical, content, AEO/GEO, competitor and action-performance views.

- Export branded HTML and PDF reports.

- Show verified external metrics separately from calculated platform scores.

- Include completed actions, measurable outcomes, unresolved risks and next recommended work.

# 8. AI Agent System

The product uses a coordinated agent system, not ten independent chatbots. A deterministic workflow engine manages state, tool permissions, evidence, retries, approvals and cost. The strategist delegates bounded tasks to specialist agents.

| **Agent** | **Responsibility** | **Key inputs** | **Primary outputs** |
|----|----|----|----|
| Growth Strategist | Creates and updates the roadmap, selects next best actions and coordinates agents. | Goals, baseline, evidence, agent outputs | Prioritized action plan |
| Keyword & Intent Researcher | Maps demand, questions, commercial intent, clusters and cannibalization. | Search data, site inventory, competitors | Keyword/topic map |
| Content Optimizer | Improves existing pages and creates briefs/drafts. | Page content, brand rules, topic map | Content changes and drafts |
| Technical SEO Specialist | Finds and resolves crawl, index, rendering, schema and performance issues. | Crawl, repository/CMS context | Technical fixes or PR tasks |
| AEO Specialist | Optimizes direct answers, FAQs, definitions, comparisons and structured answer patterns. | Queries, page structure, SERP evidence | Answer-ready sections |
| GEO Specialist | Improves entity clarity, citation readiness, factual support, freshness and AI parseability. | Content, entity graph, source quality | GEO recommendations and assets |
| Authority & Outreach Manager | Finds legitimate authority opportunities and prepares outreach. | Brand, competitors, domains | Prospect list and drafts |
| Analytics Manager | Builds baselines, evaluates actions and identifies anomalies. | GSC, analytics, rank, conversions | Performance evaluation |
| Competitive Intelligence Agent | Tracks competitor pages, keywords, changes, citations and gaps. | Competitor crawl and search data | Gap and response plan |
| Content Quality Auditor | Checks usefulness, originality, evidence, brand fit, safety and AI-readability. | Drafts and policy rules | Quality verdict and required revisions |

## 8.1 Agent execution requirements

- Every run has a unique ID, website scope, objective, budget, model configuration, tool permissions and deadline.

- Agents can access only data and tools authorized for the account and website.

- Outputs must reference evidence and structured source identifiers.

- High-impact actions must pass rule-based and specialist validation before approval.

- The system must support retries, partial failure, cancellation, idempotency and resumable execution.

- Model selection must be configuration-driven by task type, plan, cost, latency and data sensitivity.

- Prompt templates, scoring models and policies must be versioned.

- The platform must record token/provider cost per run and deduct entitlements atomically.

# 9. Continuous Improvement Engine

Continuous improvement is the product's central capability. The system maintains an opportunity backlog and selects the next best action based on fresh evidence, account goals, implementation capacity and prior results.

## 9.1 Operating cadence

| **Cadence** | **Typical work** |
|----|----|
| Daily | Availability, crawl/index anomalies, broken pages, critical technical changes, ranking/traffic anomalies and failed deployments. |
| Weekly | Opportunity reprioritization, content refresh candidates, internal links, competitor changes and distribution drafts. |
| Monthly | Content calendar, topic-cluster expansion, authority opportunities, conversion review and performance report. |
| Quarterly | Full strategy reset, content pruning, architecture review, competitor benchmark and plan adjustment. |

## 9.2 Next-best-action selection

The scheduler should consider:

- Business goal alignment and conversion relevance.

- Expected impact and confidence.

- Time sensitivity, such as technical failure or seasonal opportunity.

- Effort, dependency and user capacity.

- Risk and approval requirement.

- Content saturation and cannibalization risk.

- Previous action results for the website and similar pages.

- Plan entitlement, model cost and provider limits.

## 9.3 Experiment lifecycle

| **Stage** | **Requirement** |
|----|----|
| Hypothesis | Define why a proposed change should improve a target metric. |
| Baseline | Capture pre-change metrics and known confounders. |
| Implementation | Publish one controlled change or a documented bundle. |
| Observation | Wait for a configurable period based on action type and data volume. |
| Evaluation | Compare target and guardrail metrics with confidence indicators. |
| Decision | Keep, revise, extend or rollback. |
| Learning | Update strategy weights and recommendation history. |

## 9.4 Content growth strategy

- Prefer refreshing a valuable existing page when it can satisfy the opportunity better than creating a new URL.

- Create new content only when it serves a distinct intent, entity, stage or geographic need.

- Build topic clusters around products and services rather than random daily posts.

- Repurpose approved assets into relevant distribution formats.

- Maintain freshness using material updates, not superficial date changes.

- Prune, consolidate or redirect low-value and cannibalizing content when evidence supports it.

| **Level** | **Behavior** |
|----|----|
| Level 0 - Suggest only | Generate recommendation and export. No external write access. |
| Level 1 - Draft | Create CMS draft, repository patch or scheduled-post draft. |
| Level 2 - Approval publish | Publish only after an authorized user approves the exact version. |
| Level 3 - Policy-based automation | Auto-publish narrowly defined low-risk changes under account rules. |

## 10.2 Mandatory rollback and validation

- Store the previous value and version for every externally applied change.

- Validate page availability, canonical, robots directives, structured data and critical UI after deployment.

- Support one-click rollback where the integration permits it.

- Prevent concurrent edits from overwriting newer user or repository changes.

- Require manual approval for robots.txt, canonical, redirects, deletions, large-scale content and high-volume outreach.

# 14. Technical Architecture

## 14.1 Recommended technology stack

| **Layer** | **Recommendation** |
|----|----|

| Knowledge graph | Optional Neo4j in later phases for complex entity/topic relationships. |
| Storage | S3-compatible object storage. |
| Identity | OIDC; reuse IITDEVELOPER IAM where ready. |
| Notifications | Reuse IITDEVELOPER GNS. |
| Observability | OpenTelemetry, metrics, structured logs and traces. |
| Deployment | Containerized services with CI/CD and environment separation. |

## 14.2 Logical services

- Web frontend and backend-for-frontend.

- Account, subscription and entitlement service.

- Website onboarding and integration service.

- Crawler and rendering service.

- Website intelligence and indexing service.

- Opportunity/scoring service.

- Agent orchestration service.

- Content and artifact service.

- Publishing and repository integration service.

- Measurement and experiment service.

- Reporting service.

- Scheduler and worker fleet.

- Platform administration service.

## 14.3 Architecture constraints

- Use modular boundaries first; deploy as a modular monolith plus independent crawler/workers if that reduces initial complexity.

- Do not create a microservice for every agent.

- All long-running operations must be asynchronous, resumable and observable.

- External API calls require retries, backoff, circuit breakers and rate-limit handling.

- Provider/model configuration must not be hard-coded.

- Customer data must never be placed directly into logs or model prompts beyond authorized context.

# 15. Security, Privacy and Governance

| **Control area** | **Requirement** |
|----|----|
| Authentication | OIDC-ready login, secure sessions, email verification, MFA for privileged users. |
| Authorization | Account and website scoped RBAC enforced in backend services. |
| Secrets | Encrypt tokens/secrets at rest, use a secret manager and never expose them to agents unnecessarily. |
| Transport | TLS for all external and internal production traffic. |
| Data isolation | Mandatory account_id and authorization filters; tests against cross-account access. |
| Agent security | Tool allowlists, scoped credentials, output validation, prompt-injection defenses and restricted browsing. |
| Publishing | Approval, versioning, diff, validation and rollback. |
| Privacy | Data retention controls, export/deletion requests and processor/provider disclosure. |
| Audit | Immutable security, approval, billing and external-write events. |
| Compliance readiness | Terms, privacy policy, DPA support, consent records and regional data controls as market requires. |

## 15.1 AI and content governance

- Label generated content and retain provenance internally.

- Require citations or source references for factual claims when appropriate.

- Support industry-specific restricted claims and mandatory disclaimers.

- Prevent impersonation, fabricated testimonials, fake credentials and deceptive content.

- Allow users to exclude sensitive pages and fields from model processing.

- Provide a provider/data-residency policy before enterprise use.

| Localization | English first; architecture supports additional UI/content languages and regional search markets. |

# 17. Analytics and Measurement

## 17.1 Data classification

| **Class** | **Meaning** |
|----|----|
| Verified external data | Metrics received from connected sources such as Search Console or Analytics. |
| Observed data | Sampled search or AI-answer observations recorded at a specific time and context. |
| Calculated platform score | A transparent internal calculation such as technical health or citation readiness. |
| AI recommendation | A model-generated interpretation, forecast or suggested action. |

## 17.2 Dashboard metrics

- Technical health, indexability, schema coverage and page-quality trends.

- Search impressions, clicks, CTR, average position and query/page movement.

- Rank observations for configured queries and markets.

- Conversion events, leads, calls, bookings or revenue where connected.

- Content inventory, freshness, topical coverage and refresh backlog.

- AEO readiness, GEO/citation readiness and sampled AI visibility.

- Action throughput, approval, execution, validation and win rate.

- Competitor gaps and meaningful change alerts.

- Usage, model cost and subscription consumption.

## 17.3 Attribution limitations

The platform must communicate that search and conversion outcomes are influenced by seasonality, competition, algorithm changes, brand activity, paid campaigns and external events. Action evaluation should use baselines, guardrails and confidence indicators rather than claiming absolute causation.

# 19. Acceptance Criteria

| **Criterion** | **Definition of done** |

# 20. Risks and Mitigations

| **Risk** | **Impact** | **Mitigation** |
|----|----|----|
| Unsupported ranking promises | Loss of trust and legal/marketing risk. | Use realistic language, evidence classes and no guarantees. |
| Low-quality scaled content | Search penalties, brand damage and wasted crawl budget. | Quality gates, human approval, duplication checks and topic strategy. |
| Dangerous auto-publishing | Indexing, UX or revenue impact. | Draft/PR first, scoped policies, validation and rollback. |
| Provider/API limitations | Missing data, cost spikes or broken workflows. | Adapters, fallback providers, caching, quotas and graceful degradation. |
| Prompt injection from crawled pages | Agent manipulation or data leakage. | Treat crawled content as untrusted, isolate tools and validate instructions. |
| Cross-account data leakage | Critical privacy/security incident. | Strong account scoping, tests, encryption and audit. |
| Inaccurate AI visibility claims | Misleading dashboard. | Sampled observations with exact query, engine, time and limitations. |
| Excessive operating cost | Poor SaaS margins. | Plan limits, model routing, caching, cost ledger and job estimates. |
| Attribution ambiguity | Users may reject product value. | Action-level experiments, baselines and transparent confidence. |
| Complex initial build | Slow launch and unstable architecture. | Modular MVP, one CMS/repository integration and bounded agent workflows. |
